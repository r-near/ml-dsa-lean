# ML-DSA verify: the stack-offset slowdown found and fixed, 2026-11-07

Branch `fv-p6` (from `fast-verify` at 3c709e1). Two changes to the AVX2 dispatch wrappers (`src/fast_verify/dispatch.rs`),
both plain safe Rust around the same proved functions: every wrapper realigns its stack frame to 32 bytes, and the prepared
key is built in place. The one-shot verify, `verifying_key` and prepared-key verify no longer depend on where the caller's
stack happens to be (before: up to 7, 13 and 24 µs above the median for 44, 65 and 87, at about one offset in seven). No Lean statement or proof changed.
`tr = H(pk, 64)` in the spare lanes of the 4-way ExpandA (task 2) was not done; the reason and an estimate are below. The
checkct driver now includes the real `src/fast_verify.rs` (task 3).

Built and run as in [2026-11-07-p5](2026-11-07-p5.md): nearcore profile and target features (no AVX2), one pinned core of
an AMD EPYC 9B14 (Zen 4, KVM guest, zero steal), cpu 45 (rustc 1.99.1) and cpu 46 (rustc 1.95.0, nearcore's), load about 1
(the benchmark itself, plus another job on the box). Medians of 15 rounds, microseconds. Raw logs (build-tool lines and local
paths removed) are in the directory [`2026-11-07-p6/`](2026-11-07-p6/).

## Diagnosis (task 1)

**No hardware counters were available.** The box is a KVM guest without a PMU: `perf stat -e` on any event, including
`cycles`, reports `<not supported>`, with or without root, so `ld_blocks_partial.address_alias`, `mem_inst_retired.split_loads`
and the others the brief lists could not be read. What was used instead: `perf record -e cpu-clock` on a slow and a fast
padding, assembly of the wrappers, and a small assembly experiment.

- LLVM spills the 25 Keccak lane vectors (and polynomial vectors in the rest of verify) to stack slots that are only 16-byte
  aligned (the frame is 16-byte aligned) and accesses them with unaligned 32-byte instructions. A 32-byte access that crosses
  a 4 KiB page boundary is a page split, which is much slower than an access inside a page. The experiment (a small assembly
  loop of 32-byte loads and stores over a block at chosen addresses, not committed) gives 415 ns for a block
  that straddles a page at a start that is not 32-byte aligned; an aligned 32-byte object cannot straddle a page. The
  permutation touches several such slots per round, so a frame that spans a page boundary at the wrong offset costs
  microseconds per call, and the offset of the frame is the offset of the caller's stack. This is what the scan shows: about
  one padding in seven is slow (19 to 24 of 257), for exactly the operations that run the 4-way permutation (the
  prepared-key verify runs none, and its worst padding is 1.07 to 1.09 times the median).
- The earlier suspicion, 4K aliasing between a store and a later load (the AMD part has no counter for it), was tested and is
  not the cause: the same experiment with the block at an aliasing distance from the round-constant table is flat.
- A second, separate hazard appeared while fixing the first (below): glibc's `rep movsb` for a large by-value copy
  (`prepare_vk_avx2` returned a 57 KB `PreparedVk<8, 7>` by value) is pathologically slow at certain source and destination
  distances. The frame realignment alone moved `verifying_key_87` from 53.6 to 69.6 µs at every padding; `perf record -e
  cpu-clock` put 36% of the samples in glibc's copy routine (33.6% in one `rep movsb` instruction), 50% in `prepare_vk_avx2`
  itself. Building the key in place removes the copy.

## The fix and what it costs the proof

`align_frame()` is an `#[inline(always)]` function that creates a `#[repr(align(32))]` local and passes it to
`core::hint::black_box`; the contents are never read. LLVM must honor the alignment of the local, so the wrapper's prologue
realigns `%rsp` and every spill slot below it is 32-byte aligned. No `unsafe`, no intrinsic, no inline asm, and the code is
under `src/fast_verify/dispatch.rs`, which is not extracted (cfg'd out under Miri, like the rest of the dispatch glue). The
preferred option of the brief, `repr(align)` on the hot array types, would change the types of extracted and proved code. A
`#[repr(align(32))]` scratch struct inside `keccak_f1600_x4` was tried: LLVM splits the state into registers and spill slots
anyway and the assembly is identical, so it was dropped.

`prepare_vk_avx2` now does `let mut vk = PreparedVk::zeroed(); prepare_vk_into_x4::<K, L, Avx2>(pk, &mut vk); vk`. The proved
statement for this is `Proofs.prepare_vk_into_x4_zero` (`prepare_vk_into_x4` on the all-zero key is `prepare_vk_x4`) followed
by `prepare_vk_x4_eq`; `verify_internal_x4` already calls `prepare_vk_into_x4`. The wrapper is still a call of proved code and
the dispatch glue is still a reading of the source plus tests. The inlining check now also looks for the realignment.

## Task 2: `tr = H(pk, 64)` in the spare lanes of the 4-way ExpandA: not done

Not done this round. It is not a "would weaken the proof" item: it can be proved within the existing `Lanes4` surface. It
was skipped for proof cost and risk against the estimated gain, and nothing was written for it.

What the schedule would be. ExpandA runs `K·L` SHAKE128 sponges (16, 30, 56 for 44, 65, 87) four at a time, and every group takes
five permutations in practice (56 candidates per block, 99.9% of the 256 coefficients in five blocks). That is 4, 8 and 14 groups,
so only ML-DSA-65 has spare lanes (two, in its last group); 44 and 87 have none. `tr` is one SHAKE256 sponge of
`⌈|pk|/136⌉ = 10, 15, 20` sequential permutations (the chain cannot be split across lanes). So the schedule has to be a
"3+1": three lanes take ExpandA entries while the fourth runs the `tr` sponge across the first 2, 3 and 4 groups, with the `tr`
state saved out of lane 3 after the fifth permutation of each group and loaded back at the start of the next, which adds one
group (9 groups for 65, 15 for 87).

Estimated gain (rough; a permutation costs about 0.37 µs scalar and 0.39 µs 4-way, from `stackscan hashpk` and `expanda`): the
scalar `tr` hash costs 3.6, 5.4 and 7.2 µs (44, 65, 87). The extra group costs about 5 permutations (1.95 µs), and moving
25 words in and out of a lane per group costs 0.3 to 0.6 µs (scalar stores into the vector slots forward badly into the
32-byte loads). Net gain, about **1.2 µs (44), 3.1 µs (65), 4.6 µs (87)** for a one-shot verify, and the same for
`verifying_key_NN`; for 65 that is about 7% of 42 µs.

Proof cost: a new combined sampler-and-hasher engine next to `rej_ntt_poly_x4`, with its own loop invariants (variable rate
for the SHAKE256 lane, the snapshot of lane 3 after the fifth permutation of each group, a fixed 5-permutation group schedule
that the existing proof does not have because it runs until all lanes are done), a multi-block SHAKE256 absorb lemma, and the
reproofs of `expand_a_into_x4`, `prepare_vk_into_x4` and `verify_internal_x4`. The existing proofs of this family
(`Sample4.lean`, `ExpandA4.lean`, `Sample4Block.lean`, `FastVerify.lean`) are over 2000 lines; I estimate at least 1,500 lines
of new Lean and several rebuilds of the heavy modules. It stays the next-largest item whose gain is proof-neutral in kind
(no new trusted code).

## Task 3: the checkct driver

`checkct/driver/src/main.rs` includes the real `src/fast_verify.rs`, so a change to its exports cannot make the driver stale
(that was the failure mode of the p5 re-export list). `src/fast_verify.rs` resolves its `mod dispatch;` with an explicit
`#[path]`. `src/` changed, so `lean/Impl` was re-extracted on the box: `Funs.lean` and `Types.lean` are unchanged, only the
`extract_inputs_sha256` moved to `7cd7dfdef38482e0fedcd14ee70330a351283e4e15a224f96631edabea5c6036`, and
`python3 -I scripts/extract_hash.py check` passes. The driver builds for riscv32imac, thumbv7em and x86_64 with and without
`avx2`, in the default release profile (nightly-2026-10-06) and with the nearcore-exact flags (opt-level 3, fat LTO, one
codegen unit, abort, `+sse2,...,+pclmulqdq`) on nightly-2026-10-06 and on 1.95.0: 18 cells, all built (the x86_64 `avx2` driver
holds the AVX2 symbols and the `cpu::AVX2` cache the pre-set anchor needs, the others hold none).
