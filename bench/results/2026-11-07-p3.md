# ML-DSA verify: UseHint, Keccak rounds, lane-slice parse, fixed-width unpack, 2026-11-07

Branch `fv-p3` (from `fv-p2a`). Four verify-side speedups, each with its Lean proof; Rust and proof commits are listed below.
Built and run by `bench/fast-verify.sh` (nearcore profile and target features; `avx2` = the dispatched proved path, `v3` =
`-C target-cpu=x86-64-v3`). Same machine as [2026-11-07-p2a](2026-11-07-p2a.md) (AMD EPYC 9B14, Zen 4, KVM guest, 60 vCPUs,
core clock 3.4 GHz measured, zero steal). Pinned to idle cores (cpu 45 for the default toolchain, rustc 1.99.1; cpu 46 for
1.95.0, nearcore's). Load average 1.08 at the start of the default-toolchain run and 1.01 at its end, 1.09 and 1.00 for
1.95.0 (a load of about 1 is the pinned benchmark itself). Medians of 15 rounds, microseconds.
Raw logs: [`fast-verify-p3.log`](2026-11-07-p3/fast-verify-p3.log) and
[`fast-verify-p3-195.log`](2026-11-07-p3/fast-verify-p3-195.log).

ML-DSA-65, `avx2` column (the shipping configuration): verify 66.1 (p2a, default toolchain) becomes **54.0**; 68.1 (p2a,
1.95.0) becomes **55.4**. `verifying_key` verify 40.0 becomes 34.0 / 33.7. The scalar verify also got faster: 113.0 becomes
103.8 (default) because UseHint and `bit_unpack_pub` are plain arithmetic in both builds.
The 54.0 is 1.46 times aws-lc-rs (36.9) on this box.

## What each change saved (ML-DSA-65, dispatched verify)

Measured one change at a time with the interleaved runner `bench/ab.sh` (same binary pair alternated, pinned core; the
medians are of the per-pass medians). The sum, 12.1, equals the p2a-to-p3 difference above.

| Change | Rust commit | Saved (µs) | Proof files touched |
|---|---|---|---|
| UseHint on public data: multiply-shift HighBits, plain arithmetic, vectorized (`poly_use_hint_pub`) | 3c858c6 | 4.8 | `Proofs/Rounding.lean`, `Proofs/RoundingArith.lean`, `Proofs/Verify.lean` |
| Keccak4 rounds ping-pong (row by row into a second buffer, 12 double rounds) | ffd75cd | 1.2 | `Proofs/Keccak4.lean` |
| Lane-slice rejection parse (eight candidates per three lanes, no byte buffer) | 50eda25 | 4.1 | `Proofs/Sample4Block.lean` |
| `bit_unpack_pub`: fixed-width unpack of `t1` (10 bits) and `z` (18, 20 bits) | 2952e22 | 2.0 | `Proofs/Encode.lean`, `Proofs/EncodeKeys.lean` |

Proof commits: ca76a7c (re-extraction of `lean/Impl`), d33c836 (Keccak4, Sample4Block), e32304d (Rounding, Verify),
2f89c3a (Encode, EncodeKeys). Boundary tests for the 4-way parse: 4a0039b (`src/sampling.rs`, `boundary_tests`).

## Notes

- Under 1.95.0 two cells are slower than their neighbors and are stable across rounds (not noise): ML-DSA-65 `v3` verify
  (60.9 against 52.6 on the default toolchain) and ML-DSA-44 `avx2` `ours+verifying-key` (23.0 against 19.0). The p2a run
  showed the same kind of toolchain-specific `v3` outliers (87 `v3`: 123.8). They are code-generation differences of that
  rustc; the shipping `avx2` column for ML-DSA-65 is 55.4 on 1.95.0, above the 55 µs target by 0.4 µs.
- The 54.0 µs for ML-DSA-65 meets the 55 µs target on the default toolchain only.
- The dead-code warnings for `use_hint` and `poly_use_hint` in the bench build are expected: verify now calls
  `poly_use_hint_pub`, and the constant-time versions stay as the specification the proofs compare against.
