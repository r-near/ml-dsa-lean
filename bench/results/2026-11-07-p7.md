## The schedule

A group of ExpandA takes at least 5 permutations (256 coefficients need at least 5 blocks of 56 candidates) and the hash of a pk is a
chain of `q + 1 = ⌊|pk| / 136⌋ + 1` permutations that cannot be split across lanes. `tr_groups(n, q)` chooses how many of the first
groups take three entries and host the hash: `want = ⌈(q + 1) / 5⌉` hash the whole pk, `spare = 4⌈n / 4⌉ − n` lanes are empty in
the groups of four anyway, and when `want` is only one more than `spare` the last hashing group is not worth an extra group
(about 2.7 µs for it against 1.85 µs for the five scalar permutations it would save), so `spare` groups are used. Correctness
does not depend on the choice: whatever the lane did not reach, `tr_finish_scalar` finishes.

| Set | entries n | pk bytes | hash permutations q + 1 | `tr_groups` | groups of ExpandA (before → now) | permutations in the lane / finished scalar |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ML-DSA-44 | 16 | 1312 | 10 | 2 | 4 → 5 | 10 / 0 |
| ML-DSA-65 | 30 | 1952 | 15 | 2 (`want` = 3 = `spare` + 1) | 8 → 8 | 10 / 5 |
| ML-DSA-87 | 56 | 2592 | 20 | 4 | 14 → 15 | 20 / 0 |

ML-DSA-65's last group of four has two empty lanes, so its two hashing groups cost nothing; 44 and 87 pay one extra group each
and save the whole scalar hash (3.6 and 7.2 µs by the p6 estimate).

## Stack-offset scan (`bench/stackscan.sh check`, 257 paddings from 0 to 4096 bytes in steps of 16, ASLR off)

The 4-way hash is a new user of the 4-way Keccak's spill slots, so the p6 guard was rerun on the final tree
(`stackscan-p7-rustc199.txt`, `stackscan-p7-rustc195.txt`; `bench/fast-verify.sh` also ran its coarser 65-padding scan, `STACK SCAN OK`
on both toolchains). Largest padding over the median: 1.02 on rustc 1.99.1 (every set and operation), and on 1.95.0 1.03 for
one-shot verify and `verifying_key_NN` and 1.06 for the 87 prepared-key verify (which runs no 4-way code; 15.14 median,
16.03 maximum); no padding is above 1.1 times the median and the check passes (limit 1.15) on both toolchains.

## Tried and dropped, and skipped

| Attempt | Result |
|---|---|
| `tr_groups` returns `want` always (ML-DSA-65: three hashing groups, one extra group, the whole hash in the lane), interleaved A/B on ML-DSA-65 at pads 0 and 672, 5 passes (`schedule-variant-ab.txt`) | slower: one-shot 39.43 / 39.62 against 38.41 / 38.49 µs, `verifying_key` 29.39 / 29.51 against 28.41 µs; the extra group costs more than the five scalar permutations it saves. The committed rule (use the spare lanes only) stays |

Skipped because it would weaken the proof (estimates are rough, from `stackscan hashpk` and the p6 permutation costs):

| Item | Why skipped | Estimated gain |
|---|---|---|
| a dedicated AVX2 absorb of `pk` (loading the 17 rate words straight into lane 3 with vector loads instead of 17 scalar stores per permutation) | needs a `Lanes4` method or an intrinsic in code the proof does not model, i.e. the lane-3 insertion would be trusted, not proved | up to about 0.5 µs (p6's estimate for the scalar moves into and out of a vector slot; not measured) |
| a 4-lane split of one SHAKE256 over several lanes (tree hashing) | changes the function: `tr` must be the FIPS 204 SHAKE256 of the whole key | not applicable |
| dropping the scalar fallback (`tr_finish_scalar`) by requiring a schedule that hashes all of `pk` in the lanes | proves less: the fallback is what makes the hash correct for every schedule and every key length, and for ML-DSA-65 it is cheaper than the extra group | negative (a loss for 65, see above) |
